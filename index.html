NO SUPERHERO ORIGIN. JUST A LOT OF CODE.

It started
with a problem.

In 2009, I was working on reporting systems. The lesson was simple: when software fails, someone's real day gets harder.

Then came my first integrations. Vehicle data. Business systems. External services. Suddenly, the interesting part wasn't just the code — it was what happened between things.

Seventeen years later, that's still the job. Across lending, payments, clinical research, wealth management, telecoms and energy, I design the systems in between, and stay responsible for them once they're running.

Make it work.

Reporting systems, then my first integrations: software is only useful when it meets the world outside it.

CHAPTER 02 / SHOW, DON'T TELLAN INTERACTIVE LITTLE EXPERIMENT

Okay, but
what do
you do?

I design how services that were never meant to know each other work together: the APIs, the events, the data contracts, and what happens when one of them fails. Then I own it in production.

Here's what happens behind the scenes when you place an order, and what happens when things go wrong. It's built the way I'd build one: separate bounded contexts, an aggregate that protects the business rules, events in an append-only store, a saga coordinating payment, and a separate read model for queries. Then break it and watch it recover.
